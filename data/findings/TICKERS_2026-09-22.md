## The APR question, which is the part he actually asked

### Where the stonk.fun yield really comes from, verified

The mechanism is real and I traced it on chain today:

1. Every transfer of a reward-enabled token withholds the fee (3%, or 1% for
   STONKCAT). I read the withheld balances directly: PURR is holding
   63,890,238,378,282 raw units of withheld fees right now, ZCAT
   7,398,680,485,772, STONKCAT 45,102,169,813.
2. The authority `5KXDF6QnqhBj72hDtJNkkpFaQVUfbFXNybMsp3DiK6tD` harvests and
   swaps it. **VERIFIED: 400 transactions in a 2-minute window**, sending into
   Raydium CPMM and LaunchLab pool authorities.
3. The distributor `HuBMeYW3aDn8BH65fo8xxbP4oiexyup8udzKyccgi8Ga` pays it out.
   ⭐ **VERIFIED: 300 transactions to 4,747 distinct recipient wallets in 37
   seconds**, paying in quote assets (tokenized-stock mints such as
   `Xsc9qvGR…`, `XsoCS1Tf…`, `uniHfuPhEQ…`).

So the payout is real, automatic, and paid **in the asset the token is paired
against**, which is why PURR pays him in HYPE and why KNOTS or STONKCAT would
pay in STONK. Third-party writeups of stonk.fun describe the same design (1% or
3% tax distributed to holders in the quote asset), which matches what the chain
shows.

⭐ **But understand what funds it: other people's trading volume.** This is a
redistribution, not an emission and not a business profit. The formula is
roughly `fee rate x volume / market cap`. When volume stops, the yield stops the
same day. Nothing is accruing in the background.

⛔ **And the same tax is his cost of entry and exit.** 3% in plus 3% out is 6%
before slippage, and I measured 7.4% to 10.6% round trips. **He is behind by
roughly that much the moment he buys, and the rewards have to out-earn it before
he is level.** At LOOP's 35% round trip, no realistic reward rate covers it.

⛔ **Headline APR numbers are annualized from days.** The stonk.fun leaderboard
publishes things like a "7-day lookback modeled yearly APR" of 145.5% and an
"APY" of 327.3% assuming daily reinvestment. That is a week of volume multiplied
up by 52 and then compounded. Our own standing rule covers this exactly: never
quote a rate measured over a window shorter than the thing you are claiming. The
only number that means anything to him is **rewards actually received divided by
position value over a period he actually held.**

### HYPE: the one real protocol yield here

From Hyperliquid's own documentation (labelled DOCS, I fetched the staking page):
staking is delegated proof of stake, rewards come from **the future emissions
reserve** and not from fees, and **at 400M HYPE staked the rate is about 2.37%
per year**, falling as more is staked. Delegations lock for **1 day**, and moving
from the staking account back to spot goes through a **7-day unstaking queue**.

VERIFIED from the API today: **35 validators, 29 active**, total staked
**441,695,332 HYPE**, which is above the 400M reference point, so the rate is at
or slightly below 2.37%. Hyper Foundation validators charge **3% commission**.

So: roughly 2.3% a year, minus commission, on an asset whose price moved 4% in a
day. **The yield is not the reason to hold HYPE; it is a small addition.** And
the 7-day queue means staked HYPE is not liquid at his clip on demand.

### If any of this is LP fee APR, the impermanent loss in plain terms

Providing liquidity pays a share of trading fees but changes what he owns: the
pool automatically sells whatever is going up and buys whatever is going down.
Against simply holding, a 50/50 pool loses about **5.7% if the price ratio
doubles**, about **13.4% at 4x**, and about **25% at 10x** (standard AMM
arithmetic, `2*sqrt(r)/(1+r) - 1`). On a token that can 3x or rug in a day, that
term dominates any fee APR. ⛔ **And on a 3% transfer-fee token, every rebalance
inside the pool is taxed too**, which is part of why these pools show such wide
round-trip costs.

### Where to see real APR per token, named sources

- **stonk.fun tokens:** `thestonkboard.com` publishes per-token APR and APY,
  including a per-coin page (the LOOP-style URL pattern is
  `thestonkboard.com/coin/<contract>`). ⚠️ Treat its APR as a modelled
  annualization of a short lookback, not an earned rate. Confidence: I found
  this via web search and did not verify its arithmetic.
- **HYPE staking:** Hyperliquid's own staking page in the app, and the free API
  (`validatorSummaries` for commissions, `delegatorSummary` for a specific
  wallet's accrued rewards). This is the one place he can see actual accrued
  rewards for his own address rather than a model.
- **Any LP pool APR:** DefiLlama Yields (`yields.llama.fi`), free and keyless,
  which reports pool APR split into base fees and rewards.
- ⭐ **The only honest per-position number is his own:** rewards received in the
  quote asset, divided by what he put in, over the days he held it. That is
  computable from his wallet history and it is what I would build next if he
  wants it.

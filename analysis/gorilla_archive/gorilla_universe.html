Read this before you use any number on this page.

1. These are a third party's claims, dated, not our measurement. Every mcap figure is what Gorilla said on the day he said it. We quoted him verbatim and checked nothing about the figures themselves.

2. The contract may not be the one he meant. He posts tickers, not addresses, and 437 of these tickers are worn by more than one live token — 1,874 impersonators are named on the cards below. The services that turn a ticker into an address preferentially list tokens that still have liquidity, so a token that died tends to be invisible to them and the ticker resolves to whatever living namesake carries that symbol now. Measured strength of that effect: 86.4% [73.3, 93.6] of 44 index-absent tokens failed a live $100 round trip. Rows marked contract uncertain are the ones we refuse to score at all.

2b. Correction, 23 Sept: this page previously cited EMBER as proof that the lookup picks the wrong contract. That was backwards and is withdrawn. The resolver was right. It chose 5dvXTZ5q…, which holds $2.33m across 30 pools and sells $2,000 at 1.30% — and its first pool predates Gorilla's mention by a day. The contract analysed by hand instead, FLCr9vGM…, is a phantom: 3 pools, $1.39 of total liquidity behind a $1.31 billion claimed cap, first pool twelve days after the mention. A hand-check on chain is not automatically better than a rule; it can be on chain on the wrong account.

3. So the alive / faded badges describe the token carrying that ticker today, not the outcome of his call. They are not a track record and there is no hit rate on this page, because one cannot honestly be computed from tickers.

5. Liquidity is now summed across EVERY pool for the mint, not read from one.
